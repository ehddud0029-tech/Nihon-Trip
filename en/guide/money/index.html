Money

Cards are widely accepted in Japan, but quite a few places still take cash only.

How much cash vs. card?

Mostly cities
Card 70%Cash 30%
Lots of countryside or festivals
Card 50%Cash 50%
  • Japan's cashless payment ratio was about 58% in 2025 (Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry). Convenience stores, department stores and chains in cities almost all take cards.
  • Where you'll need cash: small restaurants and ramen ticket machines, shrine and temple fees and charms, festival stalls, rural buses, and some ryokan and traditional shops.
  • Carry around 10,000–20,000 yen in cash a day and top up at a convenience-store ATM when you run low.
  • You'll collect lots of coins, so a coin purse helps. Use them to top up your IC card or at vending machines.

Exchange and ATMs

  • Change money to yen before you leave or load it onto a travel card. Exchange counters at Japanese airports and in town usually give worse rates.
  • Seven Bank ATMs inside 7-Eleven accept foreign cards and have a Korean-language screen. AEON Bank ATMs are also widely used.
  • When paying, choose yen (JPY), not your home currency. Paying in your own currency adds about 3–8% in fees.
  • Visa and Mastercard work almost everywhere, and Japan's own JCB is especially well accepted.

Mobile pay and transit

  • Japanese QR payments like PayPay are common, and shops showing the Alipay+ logo accept many overseas wallet apps.
  • Some subways and buses let you tap a contactless card at the gate, but many lines don't yet, so an IC transit card is handier.
