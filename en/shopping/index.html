🛍️Shopping in Japan: know before you buy
Shopping in Japan ranges from department stores and malls to drugstores, electronics districts and local souvenir shops. Each city has its established shopping streets, making it easy to do it all in one neighborhood.
Top shopping streetsEach area's signature streets for department stores, fashion, electronics and subculture.
The tax-free system has changed (from November 2026)From November 1, you pay tax-inclusive prices in the store, then get the consumption tax refunded after verification at the airport when you leave — a 'refund system.' It applies when you spend ¥5,000 or more (excluding tax) at one store in one day.
💡Handy tips
  1. To get the refund, don't put purchases in checked luggage — verify them with your passport at an airport terminal before passport control.
  2. Food and cosmetics consumed or used in Japan aren't refunded, and if even one item on a receipt is missing, that whole receipt isn't refunded.
  3. Department-store food halls (depachika) are great for gift sweets, with plenty of discounts an hour before closing.
  4. Drugstore prices vary by shop. Compare two or three on the same street.

TAXFREE

Tax-free shopping changes from November 1, 2026

Instead of tax being deducted at the store, you now pay including tax and get it refunded later.

  1. Show your passport at a tax-free store and pay the tax-inclusive price
  2. When leaving Japan, customs at the airport checks your passport and purchases
  3. Once checked, the store (or a refund agent) refunds the consumption tax

Minimum spend and refund methods can vary by store, so ask before paying.
