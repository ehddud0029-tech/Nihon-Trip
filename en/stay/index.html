Good to know when booking

  • Staying within a 5–10 minute walk of a station makes luggage much easier. Check the nearest station and exit on a map first.
  • Hotel rooms in Japan tend to be small. With two or more large suitcases, check the room size (m²).
  • Cities such as Tokyo, Osaka, Kyoto and Fukuoka charge a separate accommodation tax. Check whether it's included in your booking.
  • Rooms fill up months ahead for cherry blossoms, autumn leaves and big festivals. Once your dates are set, book a refundable rate early.

Tokyo

For a first visit, Shinjuku or the Tokyo Station area is a safe choice.

Shinjuku

Best for First-timers who value convenient transport above all

Pros
The most rail lines meet here, with airport buses too. Plenty to eat and do late into the night.
Cons
The station is huge and confusing, and the Kabukicho area gets rowdy at night.

Shibuya

Best for Shoppers and night-view lovers

Pros
Close to shopping and trendy food, and within walking distance of Harajuku and Omotesando.
Cons
Rooms are pricey and weekends are very crowded.

Ueno & Asakusa

Best for Budget travelers and those using Narita Airport

Pros
Rooms are relatively cheap, and the Skyliner to Narita Airport leaves from Ueno.
Cons
Western areas like Shibuya and Shinjuku are 30+ minutes away, and it quiets down early at night.

Tokyo Station & Ginza

Best for Those making day trips or wanting a quiet base

Pros
Great access to the shinkansen and airports, with tidy streets. Handy for day trips to Hakone or Kamakura.
Cons
Expensive, and many shops close early in the evening.

Osaka & Kyoto

Many people stay in Osaka and day-trip to Kyoto and Nara. If you're spending several days in Kyoto, stay there.

Namba & Shinsaibashi

Best for First trip to Osaka, or anyone here for the food

Pros
Direct from Kansai Airport on the Nankai Line, with Dotonbori's food right outside.
Cons
Crowded and noisy late into the night, and a little farther from Kyoto than Umeda.

Umeda (Osaka Station)

Best for Those going back and forth to Kyoto and Kobe

Pros
JR, Hankyu and Hanshin lines meet here, making day trips to Kyoto, Kobe and Nara easy. Lots of department stores and restaurants.
Cons
The stations and underground malls are a maze at first.

Tennoji & Shinsekai

Best for Budget travelers using JR to Kansai Airport

Pros
Rooms tend to be cheaper, and the Haruka airport express stops at Tennoji.
Cons
The nighttime vibe isn't for everyone, and you'll need transfers to reach northern sights.

Around Kyoto Station

Best for Those with lots of luggage or several days in Kyoto

Pros
Shinkansen and airport expresses stop here, and the bus terminal makes moving with luggage easy.
Cons
You'll need a bus or train to reach major sights like Kiyomizu-dera and Gion.

Kawaramachi & Gion

Best for Those who want to soak up old Kyoto

Pros
Gion, Pontocho and Nishiki Market are walkable, and you can see the streets quiet early in the morning.
Cons
Pricey, and rooms are hard to find in peak season.

Fukuoka

The city is compact, so Hakata, Tenjin or Nakasu all work well.

Hakata Station

Best for Short trips or lots of side trips

Pros
About 5 minutes from the airport by subway, and the starting point for the shinkansen and trains or buses to Yufuin and Beppu.
Cons
Fewer nightlife options than Tenjin or Nakasu.

Tenjin

Best for Shoppers and food lovers

Pros
The shopping and dining hub, with department stores and an underground mall. Nishitetsu trains to Dazaifu leave from here.
Cons
A bit farther from the airport and shinkansen than Hakata Station.

Nakasu & Haruyoshi

Best for Those who want yatai food and evening walks

Pros
Riverside yatai stalls and night views on your doorstep, halfway between Hakata and Tenjin.
Cons
Mixed with an entertainment district, so it can feel a bit rowdy at night.

Hokkaido

Many use Sapporo as a base, see Otaru in a day, and stay at least a night in Hakodate or Furano.

Sapporo Station

Best for Those making side trips, or wanting to walk less on snowy streets in winter

Pros
Direct JR trains from New Chitose Airport, with trains on to Otaru and Asahikawa.
Cons
Susukino has far more places to eat at night.

Susukino & Odori

Best for Food lovers and night owls

Pros
Ramen, jingisukan and seafood spots everywhere, and close to Odori Park, home of the Snow Festival.
Cons
It's a nightlife district, so some areas stay noisy late.

Hakodate Station & Bay Area

Best for Those here for the night view and the Morning Market

Pros
Close to the Morning Market, the red-brick warehouses and the Mt. Hakodate ropeway.
Cons
Goryokaku and Yunokawa Onsen are 20–30 minutes away by streetcar.

Okinawa

Without a car, most stay in Naha; with one, many choose central or northern resorts.

Naha (around Kokusai-dori)

Best for Car-free travelers, short trips

Pros
Direct from the airport by Yui Rail, with plenty of restaurants and shops, so it works without a car.
Cons
The nice beaches are some distance away.

Central (Chatan & Ginowan)

Best for Road-trippers who want both sea and town

Pros
Watch the sunset over the sea at American Village, with easy drives both north and south.
Cons
Buses are inconvenient, so you really need a car.

North (Onna & Motobu)

Best for Resort lovers and families

Pros
Clear water right outside resort hotels, close to Churaumi Aquarium and Kouri Island.
Cons
A car is a must, prices are high, and it's 1.5+ hours from the airport.

Nagoya & Takayama

In Nagoya the station area is easiest for getting around; if you're heading to Takayama and Shirakawa-go, spend a night in Takayama.

Around Nagoya Sta. (Meieki)

Best for Day-trippers and short stays

Pros
Airport trains, the shinkansen and Takayama expresses all stop here, so it's easy with luggage.
Cons
Less nightlife than Sakae.

Sakae

Best for Shoppers and evening diners

Pros
Close to department stores, Oasis 21 and Osu, with lots of restaurants and bars.
Cons
You'll need the subway to and from Nagoya Station.

Around Takayama Sta.

Best for Those combining Shirakawa-go, ryokan lovers

Pros
The old town and morning markets are a 10-minute walk, and the Shirakawa-go bus terminal is right by the station.
Cons
Shops close early and nights are quiet; rooms fill fast in peak season.

Hiroshima

Most stay near Hiroshima Station or downtown and visit Miyajima as a day trip or with one night in a ryokan.

Around Hiroshima Sta.

Best for Those also visiting Onomichi or Kurashiki

Pros
Airport buses, the shinkansen and JR trains to Miyajima all leave from here.
Cons
The Peace Park is about 20 minutes away by streetcar.

Hatchobori & Hondori

Best for Sightseeing and dining downtown

Pros
Walking distance to the Peace Park, close to okonomiyaki spots and shopping streets.
Cons
You'll need a streetcar or taxi with luggage from the station.

Ryokan on Miyajima

Best for Couples and those wanting a special night

Pros
See the shrine in the quiet evening and early morning after the day-trippers leave.
Cons
Pricey, and few restaurants open in the evening.

Shikoku

In Matsuyama, stay at Dogo Onsen or downtown; in Takamatsu, near the station by the port.

Dogo Onsen

Best for Hot-spring lovers

Pros
Stay at a hot-spring ryokan and stroll to the Honkan and the arcade in yukata.
Cons
It's 20+ minutes by streetcar from the airport bus stop or JR station.

Matsuyama Okaido

Best for Budget-minded travelers

Pros
Close to the castle ropeway and arcades, with plenty of affordable business hotels.
Cons
No hot-spring atmosphere.

Around Takamatsu Sta. & port

Best for Island-hoppers and Kotohira day-trippers

Pros
Ferries to Naoshima, Kotoden and the airport bus are all close by.
Cons
The nightlife area is a bit away, around Kawaramachi.
